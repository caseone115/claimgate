Detectors answer the wrong question

They tell you a text was AI-written. Nobody was ever blocked from publishing over that. The question that decides whether an asset ships is whether this claim can be traced to something you can point at.

"No source, no claim" — enforced

Most style guides already say this. Nothing checks it, so under deadline pressure it erodes. ClaimGate makes it mechanical, and makes the build fail when it is broken.

Three checks, one exit code

Every finding quotes the text it is about, says what is missing, and says what to do. A gate an editor cannot argue with is a gate they turn off — so nothing here is a black box.

🔍

1. Substantiation

Every checkable claim is extracted and adjudicated against your evidence. Any number in a claim must appear in the evidence — that check is a string search, not a judgement call, so a fabricated figure cannot be talked into a pass.

📋

2. Policy

Your prohibited and restricted claims, written down once and enforced on every draft: guarantees, medical and financial claims, unsourced superlatives, named competitors, personal data.

🏷️

3. Disclosure

A customer-facing AI-assisted asset with no AI disclosure is non-compliant however good the copy is. Checked as a property of the draft, not left to whoever uploads it.

Relying on the human-review exemption? Article 50(4) exempts AI-assisted published text from labelling only where the content had human review or editorial control and a named person holds editorial responsibility — and the Commission’s July 2026 guidance says the responsible person’s identity and contact details must be publicly available.
